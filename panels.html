Metal Panel Layout

Enter each facet from field dimensions. A facet is one flat face of roof or wall, seen from in front with the eave or base at the bottom. Pick a template and type its sizes; the preview follows every keystroke. Slope mode takes dimensions as taped on the roof. Plan + pitch takes horizontal dimensions and stretches everything square to the eave by the slope factor.

Lengths can be typed any way: 126, 126 1/2, 10'-6 1/2", 10.5 ft, 3.2 m, or a sum like =96+24. What a bare number means, and how lengths and areas are shown, are project settings. Pitch takes 6:12, 26.6 deg or 50%.

Edges are typed from the shape and can be changed by clicking the edge on the drawing. Each type takes its allowance from the package, and any one edge can override it. Eave, valley and wall-base allowances push the panel past the line; ridge, headwall, hip and top allowances cut it back; rake, sidewall, jamb and corner allowances pull the layout in from the edge, which changes the edge rip and not the length. The dashed line on the drawing is where the metal ends.

Panel IDs read R2-07B: facet R2, seventh strip from the start edge, second piece up from the eave. A strip made in one piece has no letter. IDs belong to a location and never change when a list is re-sorted.

Long runs are split when they pass the profile's maximum length, and the pieces together are longer than the run by one lap per joint. On standing seam every transverse seam is marked as needing a splice. Laps can line up across the facet, stagger on alternate strips, or snap to purlin lines.

Waste is coverage area less roof area, broken down by where it comes from: allowances, laps, angle cuts and notches, edge rips, and rounding.

Packages group facets ordered together and hold their defaults. Profiles are a project library; the starter set is generic and must be checked against the manufacturer.

The project is kept in this browser as you work. Save writes a file to keep or send. Nothing here checks structure, wind uplift, clips or fasteners.
