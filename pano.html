    360 Photo Viewer

    Look around a 360° photo from an Insta360 or any other 360 camera, and save ordinary photos of any part of it. Point it any direction at any zoom, straighten the horizon, and save in landscape, square or portrait at as many pixels as the photo holds. The wide lens and the little-planet view are for the overview shot.

    Opening a photo

    • Use a JPEG exported as a 360 photo from the Insta360 app or Insta360 Studio, or from any other 360 camera's software. The camera's own .insp files hold the two lenses unstitched and won't look right; export them first.
    • Choose the file, drop it on the viewer, or paste it. A partial panorama works too. If the photo says how much of the sphere it covers, that is used; otherwise it's taken to go all the way round, centered on the horizon. If the camera app saved a starting direction, the photo opens facing it.
    • Nothing is uploaded. The photo is opened on this computer and isn't kept: open it again next time. The viewer needs a browser with WebGL 2.

    Framing the shot

    • Standard keeps straight lines straight, like an ordinary camera. Wide fits much more in, but bends straight lines.
    • Heading, Tilt and Level can be typed. Level straightens a horizon the camera held at an angle. Little planet looks straight down with the wide lens. Reset view goes back to the front of the photo.
    • The readout above the viewer shows the heading, the tilt and how wide the view is, in degrees.

    Snapshots

    • Frame sets the snapshot's shape, and the viewer shows that frame. Fill the viewer uses the window's own shape.
    • Size: Match the photo saves as many pixels as the photo holds at the current zoom, so zooming in gives fewer pixels, never a blurred picture. A fixed size larger than that enlarges the picture, and the note under Size says when it does. JPEG has a quality setting; PNG is lossless and much larger.
    • A snapshot is drawn at its full size from the photo itself, not copied from the screen, so what's in the frame is exactly what's saved. Its file name gives the photo's name, the heading, the tilt and the zoom.
    • Every snapshot is listed under Snapshots. Click one to go back to that view, or save it again. The list lasts until the page is closed. Only the snapshot settings are remembered between visits.
