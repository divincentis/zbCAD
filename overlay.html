2Floor plan

PDF, JPEG, PNG or TIFF. Crop off the title block and border so only the building is left.

3Make the plan stand out

Raise it if grey shading or speckles remain. Lower it if faint lines go missing.

4Line it up

  • Drag the plan to move it.
  • Corner grips make it bigger or smaller.
  • Side grips stretch it one way only.
  • The round grip turns it.

Click a corner on the plan, then the same corner on the roof. Do the same for a second corner, then fine-tune with the grips.

The arrow keys nudge the plan; hold Shift to nudge further. Scroll to zoom in for a closer match. Hold Shift while dragging a corner grip to switch proportions on or off for that drag.

5Mark locations

Click a spot on the plan, such as a ceiling stain or a room, and the marker shows where it is on the roof. Drag a marker to move it.

    Roof Overlay

    Lays a building's floor plan over an aerial photo of its roof, so you can show where a leak, a ceiling stain or a room inside the building is on the roof. It reads PDF, JPEG, PNG and TIFF plans, can clear a plan's white background and recolor its lines, and lines the plan up by two matching corners or by dragging grips. A plan that isn't to scale can be stretched one way. The result saves as a marked-up image with your notes listed under it.

    The six steps on the left walk through the job in order. This covers what they don't.

    Photos and plans

    • Drop a file anywhere, or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V. Dropping onto step 1 or step 2 says which one it is. Otherwise a PDF always goes in as the plan, and a picture is the roof photo until there is one, then the plan.
    • A PDF with several pages shows a Page list. Opening a PDF or TIFF needs an internet connection the first time, to load its reader.
    • The roof photo is never moved or stretched; the plan is placed on top of it. Crop plan only hides part of the plan, and Show whole plan brings it back.

    Lining up

    • Line up by two points sets the plan's position, turn and size in one go, but keeps its width-to-height ratio. Two corners far apart give the closest match. If the drawing isn't to scale in one direction, finish with a side grip.
    • The Roof – Plan slider above the picture fades between the two, for checking the match. Lock the plan in place once it's right, so a stray drag can't move it.
    • Markers are pinned to the plan, so they follow it if you adjust the plan later.

    Files

    • Your work, images included, is kept in this browser until you press Start over.
    • Save image writes a JPEG of the roof photo with the plan and markers on it, and the title and notes listed underneath, sized to print sharp. Set Plan visibility to 0 for markers on the bare roof.
    • Print sends the same image to the printer. To get a PDF, choose Save as PDF there; it's named after the title.
    • Save project writes one .json file with both images and everything else in it. Open it with Open project, or drop it on the page, on any computer.

    The overlay is only as accurate as its sources. Aerial photos lean, and plans aren't always as built. Confirm a location on the roof before opening anything up.
